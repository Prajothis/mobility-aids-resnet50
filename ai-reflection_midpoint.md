### Benefits

One of the biggest advantages of using GenAI was how quickly we could get something working and see initial results. This significantly lowered the barrier to getting started with the analysis.

Having a working starting point made it easier to understand the task, explore the dataset and develop ideas about which directions to investigate further. Instead of spending a lot of time implementing everything from scratch, we could quickly experiment with different analytical approaches and visualizations.

### Challenges and Limitations

However, this initial productivity boost also came with some disadvantages.

When larger parts of the code and analysis scripts were generated by AI, it sometimes became difficult to maintain a complete understanding of how everything worked internally. When something stopped working, identifying the source of the problem could be challenging. It was also more difficult to explain specific errors, make targeted changes or modify the implementation exactly as intended.

Another challenge was that GenAI sometimes tended to follow a particular direction once an approach had been established. Even when we wanted to explore a different idea or change the implementation more fundamentally, it could be difficult to guide the AI away from its previous approach.

This made it important to actively question the generated solutions, understand the underlying code and maintain control over the analytical direction rather than simply accepting the suggested implementation.

### Main Learnings
For future projects, I think GenAI can be particularly useful for gathering initial ideas, providing basic starting points and accelerating the development of our own thoughts. It helps bring ideas to life much faster and makes it easier to explore different directions without having to implement everything from scratch.

I would describe this using the 80/20 rule: through a few prompts, you can often achieve the first 80% of a task relatively quickly. You get something working, see initial results and develop a much better understanding of the possibilities.

However, getting the remaining 20% right – making the implementation work exactly as intended, understanding the underlying code, debugging problems and refining the details – can easily require 80% of the actual effort.

This is why I see GenAI primarily as a tool to accelerate the initial development and support my own ideas, rather than something that should take over the entire process. The key is to use the speed it provides without losing control or understanding of the work.
